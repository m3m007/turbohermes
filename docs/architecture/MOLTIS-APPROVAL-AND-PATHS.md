## 3. Verdicts

**Approval requirements.** BlockCell already has the shape of this: `ADMISSION_STAGES` (`tool_pipeline.rs:155-165`) runs an eight-stage pipeline whose `Policy`, `DangerousOperation` and `ExplicitConfirmation` stages are exactly the concern Moltis puts in `ApprovalManager::check_command` (`approval.rs:523-630`), and BlockCell's version is arguably better placed because it applies to every tool, not just exec, and it discloses the execution environment to the approver before the user confirms (`tool_pipeline.rs:726-733`). What BlockCell does not have is the *tiering*: Moltis separates "when do I ask" (`ApprovalMode`) from "what is the default posture" (`SecurityLevel`) from "what may never run" (`DANGEROUS_PATTERN_DEFS`, a hard floor that denies even in `Off` mode, `approval.rs:606-620`), and from "what may skip the question" (`SAFE_BINS`, operator `allowlist`). BlockCell has one boolean-ish knob (`ApprovalPolicy::Ask` vs `WorkspaceAuto`, `config/tools.rs:37-41`) and a `requires_human_confirmation` predicate, so a `WorkspaceAuto` deployment has no way to say "these twelve commands are always fine, these eight are never fine". The pending-request object is the second real difference: Moltis's approvals are named, enumerable per session, resolvable by id from the gateway RPC and from the originating chat channel (`gateway/src/approval.rs:27-146`, `gateway/src/methods/dispatch.rs:318`), and unresolved requests fail closed on timeout. BlockCell's is an anonymous inline `oneshot` scoped to the current call (`tool_pipeline.rs:1055-1096`), which is fine for a synchronous desktop confirm and unusable for an asynchronous channel one.

**Filesystem path restrictions.** This capability is largely already ported, and in two respects BlockCell is ahead. `PathPolicy::evaluate` (`crates/core/src/path_policy.rs:250-301`) does what `FsPathPolicy::check` (`moltis/crates/tools/src/fs/shared.rs:364-391`) does, plus two things Moltis has no equivalent for: a built-in secret-path deny list that is independent of user configuration (`path_policy.rs:133-152`, `~/.ssh`, `/etc/shadow`, `/etc/ssl/private`, ...), and most-specific-prefix-wins resolution between deny and allow (`path_policy.rs:279-287`) rather than deny-always-wins. Moltis's deny-wins is simpler and safer, and is worth noting, but BlockCell's extra specificity does not lose the safety property because the built-in list is checked first and short-circuits. Read-before-write and mtime-drift detection are also already present and stricter than Moltis's: BlockCell records a full `FileVersion` (length plus mtime) and refuses on any mismatch (`tool_pipeline.rs:854-935`), whereas Moltis stores `Option<SystemTime>` and treats an unstat-able file as proceed (`shared.rs:530-536`). The genuine gaps here are narrower: BlockCell has no `deny_paths`-style deny list for fs tools at all (it has deny *rules* in the policy file, which is a different and stronger mechanism, but no per-tool glob override), no equivalent of `reject_if_symlink` (BlockCell canonicalizes through symlinks instead of refusing them, `path_policy.rs:389-409`), and no config-time validation that path patterns are absolute (`moltis/crates/config/src/validate/semantic.rs:273-295`). Also note Moltis rejects relative paths at the boundary (`shared.rs:258-269`) while BlockCell silently joins them to the workspace (`path_security.rs:421-431`); that is a deliberate difference in favour of ergonomics, but it means a path-policy rule written as `~/x` and a model call passing `x` are evaluated against different roots.
